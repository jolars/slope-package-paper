\begin{letter}{Journal of Statistical Software}
  \opening{Dear editor,}

  We are pleased to submit our revised manuscript titled ``Efficient Solvers for
  SLOPE in R, Python, Julia, and C++'' for consideration for publication in the
  Journal of Statistical Software.

  This is the third resubmission of a manuscript that was originally submitted
  as number 6061 in November 2025, then as number 6222 in February, and finally
  as number 6420 in April. We are thankful for the feedback on our previous
  submission and apologize for the recurring issues encountered in the
  submissions.

  Since the last submission, we have addressed the main problem with installing
  the reproducibility materials by contributing the R package \texttt{SLOPE} to
  the conda-forge channel and updating all the Benchopt benchmarks to now use
  conda-forge for installation. We have verified that this works on Windows,
  Linux, and MacOS on both cloud and local machines and hope that this will now
  resolve the installation issues that were previously encountered.

  We have also updated all of the software packages, which include performance
  improvements and a critical bug fix in the computation of the duality gap for
  the SLOPE problem. The paper has also been updated with this new, fixed
  duality gap computation. The benchopt benchmarks have also been updated and
  all of the benchmarks have been rerun with the new software versions.

  Again, we apologize for the inconvenience caused by the previous submissions
  and hope that you will find the current submission to be complete and ready
  for review.

  This paper presents a comprehensive suite of software packages implementing
  efficient solvers for Sorted L-One Penalized Estimation (SLOPE) across four
  programming languages: R, Python, Julia, and C++. The packages support
  generalized linear models with various loss functions and handle diverse data
  structures including dense, sparse, and out-of-memory matrices. Our
  implementation features a highly efficient hybrid coordinate descent algorithm
  and we provide comprehensive benchmarks to showcase its performance as well as
  instructive examples on how to use our software in practice.

  We believe this work makes a significant contribution to the statistical
  software community by providing high-quality, well-documented, and performant
  implementations of SLOPE that are readily accessible across multiple
  programming environments. The packages are already available through standard
  package repositories (CRAN, PyPI, and the Julia General Registry) and have
  been designed with reproducibility and ease of use in mind.

  The submission includes the complete manuscript, all source code for the
  packages, and replication materials that allow readers to reproduce all
  figures and results presented in the paper.

  We confirm that this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not
  under consideration by any other journal. All authors have approved the
  manuscript and agree with its submission to JSS.

  Thank you for considering our manuscript. We look forward to your feedback and
  hope that you will find our revised submission suitable for publication in the
  Journal of Statistical Software.

  \closing{Yours sincerely,\\[1ex]
    Johan Larsson, Ma\l{}gorzata Bogdan, Krystyna Grzesiak, Mathurin Massias,
    and Jonas Wallin
  }
